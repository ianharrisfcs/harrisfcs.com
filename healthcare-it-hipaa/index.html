Security in practice

HIPAA security is more than having policies on file.

A written policy is useful only when the practice's actual systems and procedures support it. The HIPAA Security Rule addresses administrative, physical, and technical safeguards; the right implementation depends on your practice and its risk analysis.

Administrative safeguards

Security risk-analysis support, documented responsibilities, access procedures, workforce practices, and policies that reflect the real environment.

Physical safeguards

Workstation placement and security, device handling, disposal practices, and physical-access considerations for systems containing patient information.

Technical safeguards

Appropriate access controls, authentication, audit capabilities, device protection, and safeguards for electronic patient information.

Clear responsibilities matter. HarrisFCS supports the IT and security work defined in an engagement; a practice remains responsible for its own compliance program. We address business associate responsibilities and agreements when applicable. No IT provider can guarantee HIPAA compliance through a product or checklist alone. When things go wrong

Technology problems have real consequences in a practice.

Our job is to help isolate the problem, coordinate the right people, and work toward restoring normal operations within the agreed support scope.

Clinical equipment

The imaging sensor stops working.

We investigate workstation health, USB or network connectivity, drivers, software configuration, and dependencies; then work with the equipment vendor where necessary.

Security incident

A staff member opens a suspicious attachment.

We help assess the affected systems, investigate alerts, contain potential threats, and coordinate recovery or escalation according to the response plan.

Business continuity

The practice loses access to its server.

We investigate the failure, review available backups and recovery options, and help restore access while keeping staff informed of the technical situation.

Know what's included

Ongoing IT support and one-time projects are different.

Recurring managed IT generally covers agreed maintenance, monitoring, and support for the systems you already use. Projects are defined changes such as a server replacement, office-wide workstation rollout, network redesign, or major migration.

A routine workstation issue may be support; replacing every operatory computer is usually a project. The signed agreement defines what is included, and we discuss separately billable work before proceeding.

Common questions

Questions practices ask before changing IT support.

Can you work with our existing EHR or dental software vendor?

Yes. We can investigate the underlying workstation, network, access, and operating-system issues and coordinate with the vendor. Specialized application support remains with the vendor unless specifically included in our agreement.

Does a small practice really need managed IT?

Size alone does not determine the need. Consider the number of devices, sensitivity of patient information, reliance on clinical software, available internal expertise, and cost of downtime. We can help determine whether recurring support or a focused project is more appropriate.

Will hiring HarrisFCS make our practice HIPAA compliant?

No IT provider can promise that. HIPAA compliance involves administrative, physical, and technical responsibilities across the practice. We can help implement and maintain agreed technical safeguards and support risk-analysis and documentation efforts.

What happens if we already have an IT provider?

We can review your environment and discuss a transition plan that accounts for administrative access, vendors, backups, documentation, and continuity of operations before changes are made.

Are major upgrades included in monthly support?

Not automatically. Monthly services and project work have different scopes. We explain the difference and discuss project pricing before separately billable work begins.
