Four service levels

The right level of IT management depends on the business you're running.

We don't force every organization into the same service model. Discovery helps determine the appropriate level of maintenance, support, security, and governance based on your environment and responsibilities.

Essential

The foundation
Foundational managed IT

For businesses needing dependable monitoring, maintenance, and basic support. Includes 24/7 remote and LAN monitoring, ticketing, automated patching, asset tracking, business-hours remote support, core endpoint protection, backup monitoring when purchased, and monthly patch reporting.

Preferred

Everyday business IT
The standard general-business fit

Adds secure remote access, stronger endpoint protection, human-risk intelligence, phishing simulations, security awareness training, dark-web and breach monitoring, quarterly device-health summaries, and security-posture reporting.

Premier

Security-focused IT
Security-first managed IT

Adds 24/7 managed detection and response (MDR), DNS filtering, mobile-device management (MDM), email security, identity and MFA work, password management, cloud productivity suite administration, policy management, extended-hours support, and executive risk reporting.

Strategic

Security & governance
Security plus compliance governance

Adds WISP and IRS Publication 4557 work, FTC Safeguards advisory, HIPAA Security Risk Assessment support, annual compliance review, a data-security platform, custom reporting, and dedicated virtual CISO (vCISO) advisory.

Scope boundary

Recurring service maintains the environment. Projects materially change it.

A server replacement, major migration, network redesign, infrastructure refresh, or other material change is scoped as a project rather than quietly buried inside monthly support.

MaintainMonitoring, support, patching, security controls, reporting, and recurring governance.
ChangeNew infrastructure, migrations, major deployments, redesigns, and transformations.
Understanding the investment

What are you actually paying for?

Managed IT should reflect the work required to keep your business operating, not just the number of tools on a feature list.

A small office with a few well-maintained computers may have very different requirements from a distributed team with servers, complex access needs, or regulatory obligations.

FAQ

Questions worth asking before choosing managed IT.

Is managed IT useful for a very small business?

It can be. Even a small team may depend on email, devices, accounts, and important business data. The right plan depends on the environment and the support needed.

Can HarrisFCS work alongside our internal IT person?

Yes. We can discuss shared responsibilities, escalation procedures, and a scope that complements existing staff.

Is on-site support included?

Strategic includes four on-site visits per year. Other on-site work is quoted separately at HarrisFCS discretion, subject to the final agreement.

Are Microsoft 365 licenses and backup services included?

Certain licensing, backup products, and third-party services are quoted separately. Monitoring and administration vary by tier, so we review the details during discovery.

What happens outside business hours?

Support coverage differs by tier. Essential includes business-hours remote support, while Premier adds extended-hours support. Monitoring and response services are not the same as unlimited live help desk availability.

Can we change service levels later?

We can review the service scope as your business changes. Any adjustment to services, pricing, or terms is handled through an agreed change.

Are major upgrades included in monthly support?

Not automatically. Server replacements, migrations, and significant network changes are generally scoped as separate projects unless the agreement says otherwise.
