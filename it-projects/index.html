Projects vs. ongoing support

Is this a project or part of your monthly IT service?

The simplest distinction is whether we're maintaining what you already have or making a substantial change to it. Both matter, but they require different planning and resources.

Recurring managed IT

Ongoing care for your existing environment. This is the routine work needed to keep covered systems supported, monitored, maintained, and secure under your service agreement.

Common examples: troubleshooting everyday issues, routine patching and monitoring, user support, and standard maintenance included in your plan.

Think: Keep our current systems working well.

IT projects

Defined work that changes or expands your environment. Projects usually have a specific goal, scope, timeline, and completion point. They may involve new equipment, migrations, redesigns, or substantial one-time remediation.

Common examples: replacing a server, moving to Microsoft 365, deploying an office full of computers, redesigning a network, or recovering from a major incident.

Think: Build, replace, move, or significantly change something.

How can you tell which one you need?

Ask: Are we asking HarrisFCS to maintain or troubleshoot something already covered, or to deliver a new outcome? Routine support generally falls under recurring management. Work with a defined deliverable, major change, or substantial one-time effort is generally scoped as a project.

There can be overlap. For example, supporting an existing email account may be routine service, while migrating the entire organization to a new email platform is a project. Replacing one failed workstation may involve both covered support and separately scoped deployment work, depending on your agreement.

No surprises: We'll explain the distinction and discuss scope and pricing before proceeding with separately billable project work. Your signed service agreement determines exactly what's included in recurring service.
